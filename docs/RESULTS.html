This report covers the full experimental run of ci-proto, a 167 M-parameter zero-shot probabilistic TSFM: TiRex-2 with a bidirectional Mamba-3 time mixer. It covers E1–E10, the probabilistic-forecast tests and the inference-cost study. Every number below comes from a file in results/. File paths are given so each table can be traced. Protocol deviations and bug fixes made during the run are documented in docs/PROTOCOL_CHANGES.md (sections 1–13).

Update 2026-11-27: gap reruns complete. Every cell that the CUDA-driver fault had voided in E5 and E7 has been recomputed (PROTOCOL_CHANGES §14): - E5: ci-proto at 262 k and 524 k context (both horizons). - E7: ci-proto missing data on ETTh1 and Beijing, the independent mode, the six TTA flags, and fp32 on ETTh1.

The only remaining gap is ci-proto at ≥ 1 M context in E5, which runs out of memory on the 8 GB GPU when calendar features are on. No number in this report is imputed or estimated.

Set-up. - Hardware and software: one RTX 4070 Laptop GPU (8 GB) under WSL2, torch 2.14 + CUDA 13.0. - Comparators: Chronos-2, TimesFM-3, TimesFM-2.5, TiRex-2, TiRex, Moirai-2, plus task-specific statistical and supervised baselines. - All TSFMs run zero-shot. Where a model's native window is shorter than the requested context, inputs are truncated to it. - Lower is better for every error metric. COV80 should be 0.80.

1. Headline findings

  1. Point and probabilistic accuracy is at the top of the field, but the margin is small and test-dependent. - ci-proto ranks first on the leakage-free synthetic suite (E2), the long-horizon benchmarks (E1 ETT/large LTSF) and fev-bench (among our re-runs). It is second on GIFT-Eval and best on most hourly electricity-price cells (E3). - Against the strongest comparators (TimesFM-3, Chronos-2, TiRex-2), the gap is usually 1–4 % and often inside the confidence set.
  2. Single-model claims that hold up statistically: - E2 synthetic suite: best average rank (2.65 vs 3.06 for TimesFM-3). The Nemenyi critical difference is 0.36, so the rank gap is significant. ci-proto also has the highest skill vs Seasonal Naive (0.601 [0.584, 0.617]). - fev-bench, our re-run: win rate 0.855 [0.823, 0.885] and skill 0.497. Both are above every other re-run model, including TimesFM-3 (ours: 0.734 / 0.458). - E3 hourly prices: best CRPS in 6 of 9 market × horizon cells and in the 90 % CRPS model confidence set in all 9.
  3. The cohort mode is ci-proto's best configuration on multi-series data. On the 23-market daily price panel, ci-proto[cohort] has the lowest MAE and CRPS of all 17 models at every horizon, and the lowest RMSE at 7 and 30 days. Plain univariate ci-proto ranks mid-pack among the TSFMs there. Joint mode lowers ci-proto's MSE by 0–9 % on the multivariate LTSF sets.
  4. Long context: it helps up to ~16 k steps, then stops helping or hurts. - On 1-min household power (E5), MASE is flat beyond 2 k (h = 60). For h = 1440 it is best at 16 k (0.718) and 3–8 % worse at 65–524 k (0.742–0.773). - On Beijing PM2.5 (E6), a history of 20–80 % of the training period beats the full history by 25–28 % RMSE at leads of 24–48 h. - The architecture runs at 2 M steps (E8: 3.2 s, 5.7 GB). The run does not show that accuracy keeps improving with context.
  5. Prediction intervals are too narrow. - Univariate ci-proto has the worst 80 %-interval calibration among the TSFMs on the leakage-free and prospective tests: COV80 0.62–0.74 on E2, 0.72–0.73 on E3 daily. - The cohort mode is the best-calibrated model overall (mean |COV80 − 0.8| = 0.032). - The paper should report this, and post-hoc conformal calibration is a natural add-on.
  6. Prospective 2026 window (after every model's training cut-off): - ci-proto ties for best point accuracy at h = 1 and h = 7: MAE within 0.05 % of the best model. - Its CRPS is 0.5 % behind the best at h = 1 (TimesFM-3) and 1.2 % behind at h = 7 (Chronos-2). - In the short post-release window (13 days, 2026-11-13 → 25) it is 5th–6th of the 7 TSFMs. This window is too short to support a conclusion.
  7. Covariates: - Known future covariates help ci-proto most among the covariate-capable models (bike sharing: −15 % / −18 % MASE, both Holm-significant). On bike sharing it degrades far less than Chronos-2 under noisy future covariates. - Past-only covariates hurt ci-proto significantly on Beijing (+20 % / +36 % MASE). This is a limitation to report.
  8. Cost: - At 2 k context and batch 1, ci-proto runs at 0.059 s, the same order as Chronos-2 (0.049 s), 5× faster than TimesFM-3 and 4× slower than TiRex-2. - Latency and memory grow linearly with context. TiRex-2 is nearly flat: 0.054 s and 0.36 GB at 2 M steps, against 3.16 s and 5.7 GB for ci-proto.
  9. Contamination warning for comparators. - The E2 contamination audit flags TimesFM-2.5 and TimesFM-3 on Beijing air quality. Their long-lead error is 37 % and 59 % of the median model's. - Their implausibly low E4/E6/E7 Beijing errors (e.g. RMSE ≈ 19 at every lead 1–48 h for TimesFM-2.5) should be excluded from, or flagged in, any comparison.

9. E7 — Ablations and missing data

Source: results/E7_ablations_robustness/full/summary.csv, tests_vs_default.csv. The table shows SQL.

Under random missingness it degrades +5 %, while Chronos-2 even improves. Its absolute SQL on Beijing (0.407) is, however, worse than that of Chronos-2 and TiRex-2.
  • On ETTh1 it is mid-pack: +3.5 % (block) and +12 % (random at 50 %), against +4–7 % for Chronos-2 and +5 % for TiRex-2.
  • TiRex-2 and TimesFM-3 are most robust to random missingness on the synthetic suite. Moirai-2 and Chronos-2 degrade most there.

13. Validity, limitations and open items

  • Hardware faults: the WSL CUDA driver failed deterministically on some large inputs ("device not ready / Failed to create GPU mapping").
  • The affected tasks are listed in _cuda_fatal_*.txt and handled as described in PROTOCOL_CHANGES §11–13. They count as Seasonal Naive in fev-bench, and as missing configurations for Chronos-2 in GIFT-Eval.
  • The same fault silently produced error rows in E7 (ci-proto on ETTh1/Beijing in the missing-data study, indep mode, six TTA flags) and E5 (ci-proto at contexts ≥ 262 k).
  • These were rerun on 2026-11-26/27 (scripts/rerun_gaps.sh, rerun_e5_long.sh, rerun_e7_missing.sh; old checkpoints in results/_ckpt_backup_2026112{6,7}/). All are now complete except ci-proto at ≥ 1 M context in E5 (out of memory on 8 GB), see the update note at the top of this file.
  • Reproduction gap: our harness reproduces most official fev-bench numbers to within 0.1 %. ci-proto is 2 % worse than its self-reported entry and TimesFM-3 is 6 % worse. On GIFT-Eval, TiRex-2 is 2.3 % worse than official.
  • Differences of ≤ 2 % between models should not be claimed as wins without the paired tests reported here.
  • Contamination:
  • TimesFM-2.5 and TimesFM-3 on Beijing air quality are flagged; exclude them from E4, E6 and E7 Beijing comparisons.
  • The E2 synthetic suite and the 2026 prospective window are the leakage-free evidence. ci-proto is best on the former and tied for best on the latter.
  • Cost-driven settings:
  • The large LTSF sets use stride 96, and only ci-proto has a full-history lookback there (PROTOCOL_CHANGES §9).
  • E8 uses one laptop GPU; absolute latencies are hardware-specific, and the ratios are the result to report.
  • Small post-release window: 13 days and 299 one-day-ahead forecasts. Use it only as a sanity check.
  • Calibration: ci-proto's 80 % intervals fail the Kupiec test in 64 % of sources. A conformal or quantile-recalibration layer is a likely reviewer request.
